The Ledger of Aldermere

Some time ago, in the river city of Aldermere, the king's archivist Tobias Wren had one bad habit. He read things he wasn't supposed to. One winter night, deep in the vaults under the cathedral, he found a ledger bound in black leather and gold. Every page held a name, a date, and a place, and not one of those people had been born yet.

He read one name out loud, just to hear how it sounded. The candles bent sideways, the floor filled with light, and a Roman legionary crashed through his desk with his sword drawn, demanding to know which emperor had done this to him.

So Tobias read another name. Then another. By spring the streets were full of strangers from every age: a gladiator looking for a rematch with a man dead for twelve hundred years, a 1920s gangster asking where the speakeasy was, a knight who would not take off his helmet for anyone. Nobody could send them home. The ledger only works one way.

Scholars have a word for a city with more borrowed heroes than people. They call it OverSummoned.

Tobias disappeared long ago, but the ledger is still here. It passes from hand to hand, and every new owner learns the same thing: you can't send them back, but you can put them to work. They'll dig your gold, fight your battles, and run your schemes while you sleep.

The ledger has found you now. Turn the page.
