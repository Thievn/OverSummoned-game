# The human forms: names and how they should look

This follows `docs/direction-cards.md` section 4 (two forms per character) and the "Names" part of it. The rule there is the one I am working from. This file is the list of names I have suggested so far and a few thoughts on the art.

## The naming rule

Keep the pun name. Under it, on the human card, a small "born..." line. The born name sounds close to the pun but is never the real famous person. Cleocatra's human card says "born Cleora of Canopus", not Cleopatra.

`docs/lore-brief.md` says the rule is good and the specific names are up for grabs. Agreed. Treat every name below as a suggestion.

## The suggested names

| Character | Human card says | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Florida Man | born Dwayne Pruitt | A plain, ordinary name on purpose, so the legend stands out against it. |
| Cleocatra | born Cleora of Canopus | Canopus was a real town near Alexandria. The name sounds like Cleopatra without being her. |
| Captain Blackbear | born Edmund Teague | Close to the real pirate's surname without being it. |
| Dr. Quackenstein | born Viktor Ente | Viktor for Victor Frankenstein, Ente being German for duck. The data has him in the Black Forest in 1790, so a German name fits. |
| Roxy Riot | born Roxanne Pike | A punk's real name, which is always nicer than the stage one. |
| Vesper Nightshade | born Vivien Blake | Same initial as Vesper, and an ordinary name under the stage one. |

I have drawn human samples for the first five. Vesper's human card was the original mockup.

## What the human should look like

The human form should look close to the animal. If you put the two cards side by side you should see the same person: the same outfit, the same attitude, the same colours, and some trace of the animal in the face or the pose. Keep the same backdrop, so the pair reads as one character in one place.

This also keeps the cost down. Human forms should not mean redoing all fifty. The animal art is the style anchor and the human is made to match it, so the work is "one new portrait per character", not a rebuild.

## Who can be a real person

Dean asked this earlier and I want it written down. Using a historical figure is fine when the person has been dead for a long time. The lore can stay loosely true to who they were and still invent things. The "born" names above do exactly that: they point at the real story without claiming to be the person.

For anyone recent (Vesper is London in 1982, Roxy is London in 1977) the born name is invented outright and should not be a real person.
