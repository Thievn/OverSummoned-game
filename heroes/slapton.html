The history behind Will Slapton

Golden Age Hollywood ran on contracts that would now be illegal. Studios signed actors for seven years, chose their films, changed their names, arranged their marriages for publicity, and suspended anyone who refused a role — adding the suspension to the end of the contract.

Olivia de Havilland sued Warner Bros. over exactly that practice and won in 1944. The ruling, still known in California as the de Havilland Law, capped personal service contracts at seven calendar years and broke the studio system's grip on its own stars.
