The history behind Sable Quickpaw

Venice in 1610 was a republic run on secrecy. The Council of Ten operated an intelligence network across Europe, and citizens could post anonymous denunciations into stone letterboxes — the bocche di leone, lion's mouths — set into walls around the city.

It was also the year Galileo published Sidereus Nuncius in Venice, reporting the moons of Jupiter and a Moon with mountains on it. The Venetian Senate had already given him a pay rise for the telescope, having grasped immediately what it was worth to a maritime power that wanted to see ships before the ships saw them.
