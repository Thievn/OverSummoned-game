The history behind Doctor Albrecht

The beaked plague mask is the most recognisable image of the Black Death, and it is roughly 270 years too late for it. The 1348 outbreak had no such thing. The beak was described in 1619 by Charles de Lorme, physician to the French court, and belongs to the plague waves of the 1600s.

The theory behind it was miasma: that disease travelled in foul air. The beak was packed with herbs and spices to filter what doctors breathed. It did nothing for a bacterium carried by fleas, but the waxed coat and gloves may have offered some accidental protection by keeping the fleas off.
