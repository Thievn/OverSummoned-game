The history behind Agent Sterling Stoat

Prohibition made undercover work a career. The most famous agents were Izzy Einstein and Moe Smith in New York, who got into speakeasies disguised as gravediggers, opera singers and football players, and made 4,932 arrests between them before the Bureau let them go in 1925 for being more famous than their bosses.

Chicago in 1927 was Al Capone's town, and there were thousands of places to get a drink. The law that banned alcohol never banned drinking it, only making, moving and selling it, which is one reason so few people felt like criminals.
