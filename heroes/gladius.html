The history behind Gladius Maximus

The Colosseum opened in 80 AD under the emperor Titus, with a hundred days of games. It could seat around fifty thousand people and had a canvas awning, worked by sailors, to keep the sun off them.

Gladiators were the celebrities of their day. Graffiti scratched on a wall in Pompeii calls a fighter named Celadus 'the sigh of the girls', and another, Crescens, 'lord of the maidens'. Most gladiators were slaves, and the famous ones were worth far too much to waste, so many fights ended with both men walking out.
