The history behind Gaius Marcellus

In 79 AD Mount Vesuvius buried Pompeii and Herculaneum under volcanic ash and surge deposits. The ash is also why we know so much about ordinary Roman life — it sealed bread still in ovens, election notices on walls, and a great deal of graffiti that makes clear Romans were exactly as crude as everyone else.

The Roman army of the period was less a horde than a logistics operation. A legionary marched carrying armour, weapons, tools, cooking equipment and rations, and was expected to help build a fortified camp at the end of the day's march. The fighting was the easy part.
