The history behind Frau Troffea

In July 1518 a woman recorded as Frau Troffea stepped into a street in Strasbourg and began to dance. She did not stop. Within a week dozens had joined her, within a month several hundred, and contemporary accounts describe people dancing until they collapsed.

The authorities concluded the cure was more dancing, cleared guildhalls and hired musicians to keep the afflicted moving. It made things worse. Historians still argue over the cause — mass psychogenic illness brought on by famine and disease is the leading explanation; ergot poisoning from mouldy rye is the popular one, though it struggles to explain sustained coordinated movement.
