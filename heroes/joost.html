The history behind Joost van der Bloem

Dutch tulip mania peaked in the winter of 1636 and collapsed in February 1637. At the height, contracts for a single rare bulb changed hands for sums comparable to an Amsterdam house. The most prized were not solid colours but flamed and feathered ones — an effect, nobody then knew, produced by a virus infecting the bulb.

The popular version has ruined merchants hurling themselves into canals. Recent work by historians such as Anne Goldgar found little evidence of that. Most trades were paper contracts among a fairly small circle, few were ever settled, and the lasting damage was to trust and reputation rather than to the Dutch economy.
