The history behind Kenji Shibata

By 1640 Japan had effectively closed itself. A series of edicts under the Tokugawa shogunate barred most foreigners, banned Japanese subjects from leaving, and made returning home a capital offence. Dutch traders were confined to Dejima, a fan-shaped artificial island in Nagasaki harbour, which stayed Japan's narrow window on the outside world for over two centuries.

The samurai of this era had a problem: peace. With no wars to fight, a warrior class was gradually reduced to administrators and bureaucrats, drawing salaries in rice and increasingly in debt to the merchants they officially outranked.
