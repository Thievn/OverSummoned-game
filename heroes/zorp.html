The history behind Zorp, Destroyer of Tuesdays

The phrase 'flying saucer' dates from June 1947, when a pilot named Kenneth Arnold reported nine shining objects near Mount Rainier and said they moved like a saucer skipped across water. Newspapers did the rest.

Two weeks later the army air base at Roswell, New Mexico, announced that it had recovered a 'flying disc' from a ranch, then said the next day that it was a weather balloon. In 1994 the Air Force gave a third answer: the wreckage came from Project Mogul, a secret programme of high-altitude balloons built to listen for Soviet nuclear tests.
