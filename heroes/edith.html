The history behind Edith Crane

Victorian London's great engineering achievement was not a railway but a sewer. After the Great Stink of 1858 made Parliament itself unusable, Joseph Bazalgette was authorised to build some 1,100 miles of sewers beneath the city.

He sized the pipes for the largest population he could imagine, then doubled the diameter, reasoning that this was the sort of thing one only gets to do once. That decision kept the system working for over a century longer than it had any right to.
